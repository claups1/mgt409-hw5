3. Would it be simpler as one agent?

102 would be simpler as one agent with tools. It was just rent, lease, and cash lookups, with no real back-and-forth. The team is still better for 101 and 103, where stock, money, and customer wording needed separate checks and different tool access.

Why 102 didn't need a team

  • The Boss's own first tool, review_rent_notice, already returns the lease, the payments recorded for it, cash balances, open invoices, the cash left after rent ($1,000), and the cash left after rent and invoices ($160). That is every fact the specialists later reported.
  • The specialists repeated work. Facilities and Accounting both called get_ticket and get_lease.
  • The run took 9 model requests, 13 tool calls, 2 hand-offs, and 22,944 input tokens. One agent with review_rent_notice and propose_payment would reach the same $2,400 “needs owner” proposal in fewer steps.

Why 101 and 103 did

  • Separate checks caught real issues. On 101, Inventory's propose_restock flagged invoice 501 as a likely duplicate. On 103, Accounting's propose_discount checked the below-cost limit, and Inventory checked the 12-unit shortfall and arrival date.
  • Different tool access protects customers' privacy. Customer Service writes the reply using check_inventory, which is documented as “Unit cost is NOT included”. Its draft must also pass propose_customer_reply, which flags internal figures and other customers' names. Keeping costs and cash with Accounting keeps them out of the customer's message.
  • It costs more, which is worth it here. Each of these runs took 14 model requests and 20 tool calls (37,560 and 34,879 input tokens).

4. Three problems the team could solve now

  1. “Does the white tee come in size L, and what does it cost?” (no order yet)
    • Inventory or Customer Service runs check_inventory on CC-TEE-WHITE size L. The database has 3 on hand in Aisle B at a $28 list price.
    • Customer Service drafts the answer and checks it with propose_customer_reply.
    • This is a quote, not a stock-out like 101: there's no invoice and no money, so Accounting and Facilities aren't needed.
    • The question would have to be entered as a ticket first, because propose_customer_reply takes a ticket id.
  2. Rent and a new vendor invoice land in the same week, and checking can't cover both and keep the $500 reserve
    • Accounting runs get_cash_position, which lists open invoices, rent due within 30 days, the cash left after all known bills, and a below-reserve flag.
    • It then runs propose_payment for each bill, which shows the cash left after that payment and after the other known bills, plus who must approve.
    • This situation exists right now. Checking holds $736, invoice 501 ($840) is open, and the next $2,400 rent is due 2026-10-02.
    • propose_payment doesn't rank the bills. It shows the consequences of each, and the owner decides the order.
  3. The shop needs hoodies fast for a campus event: which vendor is quickest?
    • Inventory runs list_vendors, which sorts by lead time: QuickShip CT 1 day (local courier), Elm City Gifts 3 days (mugs and small goods), Bulldog Print Co 5 days (apparel reprint).
    • Inventory's rules require a vendor whose specialty fits the product. So for hoodies the honest answer is Bulldog Print Co: 5 days is the fastest apparel option.
    • propose_restock then gives the arrival date and the cost, just like the 12-hoodie restock on 103 (12 × $22 = $264, earliest 2026-09-05).

5. Three problems it couldn't solve (and what it would need)

  1. Customer refunds or returns
    • Why it can't: none of the 18 tools issues or prepares a refund. propose_payment accepts only kind “rent” or “invoice”. The 9 tables have no orders or sales history to check what a customer bought, and nothing connects to a card processor. SHOP_POLICY already says every_refund_needs_human_approval, but no tool uses it.
    • Needs: a refund-proposal tool, an order-history table, and a payment-processor connection. Customer Service would handle the customer and Accounting would check the money, with a human approving through the same approval route as payments.
  2. Demand forecasting: what to restock before it runs out
    • Why it can't: inventory stores only today's quantity per size. There is no sales history, so there is no trend to forecast from. Today the agents can only react to a shortfall after a customer asks, as on 103.
    • Needs: a sales-history table, a forecasting tool, and an Analyst agent that suggests restocks for Inventory to check with propose_restock.
  3. Dealing with a new vendor or a price change
